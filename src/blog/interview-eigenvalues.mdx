This is a standard result, but the proof is short enough to be worth recording.

<NoteSection type="theorem" title="Eigenvalues of Real Symmetric Matrices">

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be symmetric (i.e. $A = A^T$). Then every eigenvalue of $A$ is real.

</NoteSection>

<NoteSection type="proof">

Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue with (possibly complex) eigenvector $v \neq 0$, so $Av = \lambda v$. Consider the complex inner product $\langle u, w \rangle = \bar{u}^T w$.

Compute:

$$
\langle v, Av \rangle = \langle v, \lambda v \rangle = \lambda \langle v, v \rangle = \lambda \|v\|^2
$$

On the other hand, since $A$ is real and symmetric ($A = A^T = \bar{A}^T$):

$$
\langle v, Av \rangle = \langle Av, v \rangle = \overline{\langle v, Av \rangle}
$$

Therefore $\langle v, Av \rangle$ is real. Since $\|v\|^2 > 0$, we conclude $\lambda = \langle v, Av \rangle / \|v\|^2$ is real.

</NoteSection>

<NoteSection type="remark">

The same argument, with minor modifications, shows that eigenvectors corresponding to *distinct* eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix are orthogonal. This is the starting point of the spectral theorem.

</NoteSection>
